# Round 4.3 - Source-bound consolidation of the long-term memory (design v1, for review)

Context: the five-phase plan (2026-10-20) names three parts of phase 2: versioned
statements, contradictions, and source-bound consolidation ("quellengebundene
Verdichtung"). Rounds 4.0, 4.1 and 4.1b established the first two on structured reports
(holdout PASS; a continued instance with restart). Round 4.2 (phase B, language input)
was closed under option A on 2026-10-24 (development requirements not met, holdout not
evaluated); it does not change M. Astra placed compaction after phase B and asked for it
to be tested separately, "so that it stays recognisable whether an error comes from the
resolution logic or from information lost by compaction" (reviews/2026-10-20_r40v1b_astra.txt;
docs/ROUND_4_0.md section 6). This round adds nothing to the resolution logic: M, R, the
source policy and the report schema stay exactly as in round 4.0.

Uncertainty removed: can the memory replace its growing raw report log by a compact
consolidated state - so that (a) every answer stays exactly the answer of the full
memory, including the report ids it cites; (b) every consolidated item stays bound to
the original reports it comes from and can be verified against the archive; (c) when the
old log is actually deleted, every question that would need it is answered "compacted",
never with a wrong value; and (d) a source-bound digest per fact (versions merged, every
value with its supporting and its dissenting sources) is exactly reproducible by the
independent reference?
Claim limits (fixed now): structured reports of the round 4.0 / 4.1 generators only; the
fixed source policy of round 4.0; lossless consolidation under that policy (section 7
states what it cannot shrink); no language, no retrieval budget, no summarisation by a
language model.

## 1. What stays

Report schema, ReportStore (append-only, SHA-256 chained), M (episodic_lab/r40_memory.py),
R (episodic_lab/r40_reference.py), the transition rules and the operators current,
valid_at, believed_at, why_changed, conflicts, history, before - unchanged, files not
touched. The worlds are generated by the round 4.0 generator (families P1-P9) and the
round 4.1 generator (T1-T5) with NEW seeds (r43-dev-1, r43-holdout-1), dev 10 / holdout
40 pairs per family, delays 0 / 50 / 200 / 500; the question tables of 4.0 and 4.1
unchanged, plus one digest question per world (section 3). The 4.0 conformance set (hand
worlds) is run as well.

## 2. The consolidated state (checkpoint) and the two modes

Checkpoint C(c) for a cut day c: for every fact (subject, attribute) - anchor events
included, they are facts like any other - the claims STANDING at knowledge time c, each
with exactly the fields the resolver reads: claim id (the report id that created it),
value, valid_from, rank, source, origin. Nothing else is kept: withdrawn claims, retract
reports, ineffective reports and every other report field are dropped.
Header: schema, cut day c, the seal of the archive prefix (count and head hash of the
report chain over all reports with report_day <= c), the hash of the parent checkpoint
(genesis for the first), the highest report id at c, the SHA-256 of the resolver source
(episodic_lab/r40_memory.py) and the checkpoint's own hash over its canonical JSON.
Why this is lossless for every knowledge time k >= c (proof obligation, also tested):
M's transition rules use a target claim only through "exists and stands"; a target that
is withdrawn, claimless (an ineffective correction) or missing makes a correction or
retraction ineffective in all three cases alike. So the standing claims at c determine
the standing claims at every k >= c, given the reports of (c, k]; every operator reads
only standing claims at its knowledge times and, for why_changed, the reports in
(k1, k2]. Report ids are allocated in total order (r00001 ...; generator property,
audited), so "new id" equals "id above the highest id at c" - the uniqueness rule of
round 4.1b stays checkable without the dropped reports.
Hot memory H = the latest checkpoint + the tail (the reports with report_day > c, a chain
segment that starts at the archive head recorded in the checkpoint). H uses M's transition
and version functions unchanged (imported, not copied); only its starting claims differ -
so any difference to M is a consolidation error, never a resolution error.
Second, independent check of the losslessness: every claim of C(c) is written as one
synthetic assert report (same id, value, valid_from, rank, source, origin; report_day c);
R on these synthetic reports plus the tail must answer every question with all knowledge
times >= c exactly as R on the full reports. A checkpoint is
built either from scratch (replay of the archive prefix) or incrementally (parent
checkpoint + segment); both must give the same bytes.
Mode A (archive kept as cold storage): checkpoints every D days (D = 5, fixed); a
question is answered from the latest checkpoint whose cut is <= the smallest knowledge
time the question needs (believed_at: t; why_changed: k1; all others: now) plus the
segment up to the needed time. Every question is answerable; the cold archive is read only
segment-wise.
Mode T (truncated): only the latest checkpoint C(c) and the tail exist (the archive prefix
is deleted). A question that needs a knowledge time < c is answered
{"status": "unknown", "reason": "compacted"} (why_changed: "compacted"); every other
question is answered from H. Mode T never answers a question that needs a deleted report
with anything else.

## 3. Source-bound digest

digest(s, a) at knowledge time k (= now): the history of (s, a) at k, consecutive versions
with the same status and the same value(s) merged into one segment:
{valid_from, valid_to, status, value | values, support, dissent}
- support: the deciding claims of the merged versions (id, source, rank), sorted by id;
- dissent: the standing claims with a valid_from inside the segment that do not decide
  (outranked claims; id, value, source, rank), sorted by id;
- plus the current answer at k.
Every value in a digest appears with at least one cited claim; no value without a source.
R computes the digest independently from its own table (a separate function, no shared
code with M's digest).

## 4. Cuts and cases

Per world (pair world A or B, delay): cut days = every day from the first to the last
relevant report day + 1 (exhaustive over the relevant part, so every relevant report -
including every correction, retraction, late report and anchor - lies on each side of some
cut), plus the days at 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4 of the irrelevant tail and the question day "now".
Mode T is checked at every cut; the cuts in increasing order form the chain for the
composition check; Mode A uses its fixed D. Every 4.0 / 4.1 question and the digest
question is asked at every cut (Mode T) and once in Mode A.

## 5. Systems and lossy controls

H (Modes A and T), M (full store), R (reference, digest added). Lossy checkpoint controls
(each a deliberately reduced checkpoint, answered by the same H code):
- K1 current-only: per fact only the deciding claims of the current version at c.
- K2 deciding-only: the deciding claims of every version at c; outranked standing claims
  dropped (the digest's dissent and any later re-emergence of an outranked claim are lost).
- K3 anonymous timeline: per version at c one claim with the version's value, valid_from
  and rank under a NEW id; the identity of the original claims is lost (a later
  correction or retraction of a pre-cut report finds no target; cited ids change).
A case (world, cut, question) is INFORMATIVE for a control if R, run on the control's
checkpoint written as synthetic reports (as in section 2) plus the tail, answers
differently from R on the full reports - a property of the worlds, computed by R before any run of H. Prediction: each
control fails exactly on its informative cases (on Mode T's in-scope questions).

## 6. Measures and pass rule (fixed before any run)

Pass rule on the holdout (zero allowed errors everywhere; denominators reported):
(1) Mode A: every question identical to M, report ids included, in every world; M = R on
    every question; the digest of H = the digest computed from the full store = the
    digest of R; and R on C's synthetic reports plus the tail = R on the full reports
    (section 2) at every cut.
(2) Mode T: at every cut of every world, every question that needs no knowledge time < c
    identical to M; every question that needs one answered "compacted"; no other output.
(3) Composition: for every consecutive pair of cuts, C(c2) built incrementally from C(c1)
    and the segment is byte-identical to C(c2) built from scratch; and C(c) from scratch is
    identical when built in a new process.
(4) Integrity and persistence: every tamper case is detected on load - a claim value or
    rank changed, a claim removed or added, the archive seal changed, the parent hash
    changed, a tail record changed, removed or reordered, a tail report id at or below the
    checkpoint's highest id; a checkpoint and its tail written to disk, reloaded in a new
    process, answer every question identically.
(5) Source binding: every report id in every answer and digest of H is the id of a report
    of the archive (Mode A) or of a claim in C or a tail report (Mode T), and that claim
    stands at the answer's knowledge time (checked with R).
(6) Lossy controls: K1, K2 and K3 each fail on every one of their informative cases and on
    no other case; each has >= 30 informative cases (otherwise it is reported as
    non-discriminating and does not count as evidence).
(7) The 4.0 conformance set through Modes A and T at every cut.
Measures (report only): size of C(c) (claims, bytes) against the archive prefix (reports,
bytes) per delay and cut; reports and claims read per question in Mode A / T against a
full replay; digest segments against history versions and the number of dissent entries;
time. This is the pass rule of a finite test suite (14 families x 40 pairs); zero errors
do not establish a general error rate.
Joe's check (about 5 cases, chosen by rule): a cut between a report and its correction; a
late report whose valid_from lies before the cut; a conflict opened before and resolved
after a cut; an anchored question whose anchor lies before the cut; a Mode-T answer
"compacted".

## 7. Known limits (stated in advance)

Under the round 4.0 policy any standing claim can still be corrected or retracted later,
so lossless consolidation cannot drop standing claims: C shrinks only by the withdrawn
claims, the correction / retraction / ineffective reports and the unread fields; in
worlds that are mostly assertions the gain in size is small and the gain is mainly in what
a question has to read. A real reduction of size needs a policy decision - for example a
finality horizon (claims older than H days can no longer be targeted, after which
outranked old claims could be dropped and equal supporters merged); that is a different
policy and is NOT part of this round (question to the reviewer). Questions about knowledge
times before a deleted prefix are unanswerable by construction - Mode T makes that
explicit instead of hiding it.

## 8. Cost

Model-free. Development locally; the holdout (generated and frozen before any run of H
on it) in one GitHub Actions run like round 4.0, expected minutes. Tests: lemma cases by
hand (a correction / retraction of a withdrawn, claimless and missing target before and
after a cut), composition, every tamper case, the conformance set.
